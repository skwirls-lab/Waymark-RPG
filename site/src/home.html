10 min

To make a character

Roll three attributes, pick where you come from and what you carry, and you are ready. No classes to study and no long lists to memorize.

1 roll

Runs the whole game

When something risky could go either way, roll a twenty-sided die against one of your attributes. Everything else is conversation.

0 misses

Attacks always hit

Fights are fast and dangerous, so clever plans matter more than lucky dice. Often the smartest move is not to fight at all.

Play anything

The same rules, whatever the world

Waymark does not come with a setting. It comes with the tools to run yours. A sword and a laser rifle use the same rule. So do a wizard's spell, a mutant's gift, and a robot's built-in function.

What it sounds like

The game happens in conversation

Warden The bridge ahead is rope and old planks. It sways in the wind. On the far side, a lantern is moving between the trees.

Sam Do the ropes look like they'd hold us?

Warden The ropes look sound. A few planks in the middle are gray and split.

Sam I'll cross slowly and test each plank before I put weight on it.

One person describes the world. Everyone else decides what to do in it. The rules step in only when nobody knows what should happen next.

Table or no table

Play in pure conversation, or put miniatures on the kitchen table. The movement rules scale to whatever space you have.

Getting started

Your first game, in four steps

  1. Gather two to five people. One of you will be the Warden, who describes the world. The rest each play one character.
  2. Read the Introduction. It explains the dice, the three attributes, and the one roll the game is built on.
  3. Make characters together. Part Two walks you through it step by step.
  4. Play. The Warden describes a place and a problem. Everyone else decides what to do about it.
