# WAYMARK

### A modular adventure game for any world

**Version 0.1 — first draft, unplaytested.** Working title; see `DECISIONS.md`.

---

## What this game is

Waymark is a rules-light roleplaying game for one Warden (the facilitator) and one or more players. Characters have three ability scores, a handful of Hit Protection, and whatever they carry. Attacks always hit. Danger is real and announced in advance. Most problems are solved by asking questions and making a plan, not by rolling dice.

On top of that small core, Waymark adds **modules** you can switch on: Origins, innate Powers, structured advancement, corruption tracks, item crafting, tabletop movement scales for miniatures, and genre options. A campaign states which modules it uses. Everything not switched on is simply absent, and the core game is complete without any of them.

A cairn is a pile of stones that marks a trail. A waymark is any sign that does the same job. This game grew from *Cairn* by Yochai Gal and marks trails into other worlds.

## How to use this book

| Part | Contents | Do you need it? |
|---|---|---|
| **I. Core Rules** | The complete default game | Yes. Everything needed to play. |
| **II. Optional Modules** | Self-contained add-ons | Only the ones your campaign switches on |
| **III. Workshop** | Templates for making your own Origins, gear, Powers, threats, tracks, and settings | When you want to build something |
| **IV. Setting Packs** | *Sylvanholt: The First Song* | When you play in that setting |
| **Appendices** | Quick reference, sheets, templates, glossary, the spell list, license | At the table |

**The Campaign Card.** Before play, the Warden fills in a Campaign Card (Appendix C): setting, modules in use, movement scale, and any dials. That card is the whole answer to "what rules are we using?"

**Status labels.** This is a working draft. Rules carried over from Cairn are unmarked. Rules that are new to this book are marked **[PROPOSED]** until they have been played. Unresolved questions are collected in `DECISIONS.md` rather than scattered through the text.

## What you need

- One Warden and one to five players.
- Dice: d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20. One set can be shared.
- A character record for each player (Appendix B), and pencils.
- Optional: miniatures and a ruler or grid, if you use a tabletop scale (Core Rules, chapter 6).

# PART I — CORE RULES

This part is the complete default game. A group can create characters and play using only these sixteen chapters.

---

## 1. Core principles

- **Fiction first.** The situation in the game world decides what is possible. Dice are used only when the outcome is uncertain and failure matters.
- **Classless.** A character is defined by what they carry, what they have done, and what the player chooses, not by a class.
- **Danger is real and visible.** Characters are fragile. Death is close, but it is never random and never a surprise. The Warden signals serious danger before it lands.
- **Information is free.** Players do not roll to learn what their characters can plainly see, hear, or reasonably know.
- **Informed choice.** Players should understand the likely risks of an action before committing to it.
- **Neutral Warden.** The Warden portrays the world honestly and applies the rules evenly. They are neither the players' opponent nor their protector.
- **Growth through play.** Characters change because of what happens to them in the world.
- **A shared goal.** The party works together toward common aims.

---

## 2. The play loop

1. **The Warden describes the situation**, including anything dangerous that is evident.
2. **Players ask questions.** The Warden answers honestly and directly.
3. **Players say what their characters do**, and what they hope to achieve.
4. **The Warden decides whether a roll is needed.**
   - Impossible in the fiction? It does not happen. No roll.
   - Sensible and not risky? It happens. No roll.
   - Uncertain and risky? The Warden states the stakes, then calls for a **save** (chapter 3).
5. **Resolve and apply consequences.** The world changes. Go back to step 1.

When intentions are vague, the Warden offers a choice: "So, A or B?"

---

## 3. Dice and saves

### Dice notation

`d6` means roll one six-sided die. `2d6` means roll two and add them. `d8+d8` on an attack means roll two d8 and keep the highest (see chapter 8).

### Ability scores

Characters have three ability scores, usually between 3 and 18.

| Score | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Strength (STR)** | Physical power, toughness, endurance. Forcing a gate, resisting poison, surviving a wound. |
| **Dexterity (DEX)** | Speed, balance, reflexes, stealth. Dodging, climbing, sneaking, acting first. |
| **Willpower (WIL)** | Force of personality, nerve, focus. Persuading, intimidating, resisting fear, controlling the uncanny. |

Rough scale: 3 is deficient, 6 is weak, 10 is average, 14 is noteworthy, 18 is legendary.

### Saves

A save is a roll to avoid a bad outcome from a risky action or event.

1. The Warden names the ability and the stakes.
2. Roll a d20.
3. **Equal to or under the ability score: success.** Over: failure.
4. A **1 always succeeds**. A **20 always fails**.

Only roll when there is risk. A character with time, tools, and no pressure simply succeeds at things they could plausibly do.

When two sides compete, the side with more at risk makes the save.

### Advantage and disadvantage **[PROPOSED]**

Some rules grant Advantage or Disadvantage on a save.

- **Advantage:** roll two d20 and keep the **lower**.
- **Disadvantage:** roll two d20 and keep the **higher**.

They cancel one for one. The Core Rules never require them; modules and setting packs may.

### Die of Fate

When the Warden wants pure chance (weather, whether a guard is awake, whether the shop has rope), roll 1d6. **4 or more favors the characters. 3 or less goes against them.**

## 5. Inventory and equipment

### Slots

A character has **10 inventory slots**:

- **2 hand slots** (one per hand): what you are holding.
- **2 body slots**: belt, chest, back, or head. Worn armor, a helmet, a quiver.
- **6 pack slots**: your backpack. Getting something out of a pack in a fight takes your action.

### What takes a slot

- Most items take **1 slot**.
- **Bulky** items take **2 slots**. They are usually two-handed or awkward.
- **Small** items (a ring, a key, a few coins, a vial) can be bundled several to a slot, or ignored if trivial. 100 coins fill a slot.
- Worn armor goes in body slots. A shield occupies a hand slot while in use.
- An emptied backpack can serve as an emergency sleeping bag.

### Full inventory

**A character with every slot filled is reduced to 0 HP** and cannot recover HP until at least one slot is free. They are exhausted by the load, not wounded: no Scar roll is triggered by this.

A character cannot carry more than their slots allow.

### Fatigue

Fatigue is exhaustion made physical. **Each Fatigue occupies one inventory slot** until removed (chapter 10). If you must take Fatigue and have no free slot, you must drop an item to make room. **[PROPOSED]** If you have nothing left to drop, you collapse and can do nothing until you rest.

### Carrying more

- **Cart** (+4 slots): must be pulled with both hands.
- **Horse** (+4 slots), **mule** (+6 slots, slow), **wagon** (+8 slots, slow).
- **Hirelings** can carry gear (chapter 11).

## 6. Time, movement, and distance

### Time

| Unit | Length | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Round** | About ten seconds | Combat and other moment-to-moment action |
| **Turn** | About ten minutes | Careful exploration of a dangerous site |
| **Watch** | About a third of a day | Travel and wilderness. A day has three: morning, afternoon, night |

### Acting in a round

On your turn in a round you may **move once** and **take one action**, in either order. You may give up your action to **move a second time**.

### Distance bands

Waymark describes distance in four bands. Theater-of-the-mind play uses the bands alone. Tabletop play converts them to inches or squares using a scale.

| Band | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Close** | Within arm's reach. Melee happens here. |
| **Near** | Reachable with one move. Thrown weapons reach this far. |
| **Far** | Reachable with two or three moves. Bows, slings, and crossbows reach this far. |
| **Distant** | Beyond that. Visible, but out of normal reach and range. |

### Choosing a scale **[PROPOSED]**

Pick one option for the campaign and write it on the Campaign Card. They all use the same rules; only the measuring changes.

| Scale | One move | Close | Near | Far | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mind** | One band | — | — | — | No table. Pure description. |
| **Skirmish (default for miniatures)** | 6" | 1" | up to 6" | up to 18" | Kitchen tables, 3 ft × 3 ft play areas, wargame terrain |
| **Classic** | 8" | 1" | up to 8" | up to 24" | Larger tables; matches a 40 ft move at 1" = 5 ft |
| **Grid** | 6 squares | adjacent | up to 6 squares | up to 18 squares | Battle mats. Diagonals count as 1. |

On the miniature scales, **1 inch or 1 square is 5 feet**. Any distance in this book given in feet converts on that basis: 30 ft is 6", a 10 ft radius is 2".

**Custom scales.** A Warden may set any move distance. Guidelines:

- Set one move to roughly **one sixth** of the shortest side of your play area. Shorter moves make positioning and ranged weapons matter more. Longer moves make fights fast and loose.
- Keep **Far at three moves**. That ratio is what keeps ranged weapons useful but not dominant.
- Keep Close at base contact or 1".
- Tell the players the numbers before the first fight.

### Movement details

- **Rough terrain** (rubble, undergrowth, shallow water, stairs): each inch costs two. In Mind scale, crossing it takes your whole round.
- **Climbing, swimming, squeezing**: as rough terrain, and a save if there is danger.
- **Fast and slow creatures**: the Warden may give a creature a move half again as long (9" on Skirmish) or half as long (3"). Most creatures use the standard move.
- **Moving away from a foe** does not provoke a free attack. If slipping away is risky in the fiction, the Warden calls for a DEX save.
- **Line of sight** is judged from the miniature's point of view. If in doubt, the target is in cover.

## 7. Exploration

### Dangerous sites (turns)

In a dungeon, ruin, or similar place, play moves in ten-minute turns. In one turn the party can do one thing: move cautiously through an area, search a room, pick a lock, rest briefly, and so on.

- **Searching** takes a turn and finds what is there to be found. No roll. Hidden things are found by describing where and how you look.
- **Traps and hazards** should be evident, or at least hinted. Dealing with them is a matter of description. A save is called only when a character acts on a hazard without neutralizing it.
- **Light.** A torch burns for about an hour (6 turns). A lantern burns about four hours per flask of oil. In darkness, attacks are impaired and moving safely may require saves.

### The wilds (watches)

- In one watch, a party can **travel** one stage (roughly 6 miles on a road or easy ground), **explore** the area they are in, **forage**, or **make camp and rest**.
- **Hard terrain** (mountain, swamp, deep forest) takes two watches per stage.
- **Getting lost.** Off a road, without a guide or landmark, the Warden rolls the Die of Fate each travel watch. On 1–3 the party drifts off course and must spend a watch finding the way.
- **Needs.** Each character must eat one ration per day and sleep one watch per day, or become **deprived** (chapter 10).
- **Weather** and nightfall make everything harder. The Warden describes them before the party commits.

### Event roll

Roll 1d6 when the party lingers or makes noise in a dangerous site (about every three turns), and once per watch in the wilds.

| d6 | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Encounter.** Something arrives. Roll reaction (chapter 11) if its attitude is not obvious. |
| 2 | **Sign.** A clue to a nearby encounter: tracks, noise, a smell. |
| 3 | **Environment.** The surroundings shift: weather turns, water rises, a door locks. |
| 4 | **Loss.** A torch gutters, a ration spoils, a rope frays. |
| 5 | **Strain.** The party must rest a turn (or a watch), or each takes one Fatigue. |
| 6 | **Quiet.** Nothing happens. |

### Rest

- **A short rest**: a few moments and a drink of water. Restores all HP (chapter 9).
- **A night's rest**: one full watch of sleep in a safe place. Clears Fatigue (chapter 10).
- **A week's rest** under a healer's care: restores lost ability scores.

## 8. Combat

Fighting is a choice, and rarely the safest one.

### Starting a fight

1. **The Warden telegraphs** what the opposition is about to do.
2. **Surprise.** If one side is unaware, the other side acts first. No roll.
3. Otherwise each player makes a **DEX save**. Those who succeed act before the opposition in the first round. Those who fail act after.
4. From the second round on, sides alternate: all player characters, then all opponents.

Players on the same side act in any order they like.

### Your turn

Move once and take one action (chapter 6). Common actions: attack, use an item, use a Power (if in play), help an ally, attempt a maneuver, move again.

### Attacks

**Attacks hit automatically.** There is no roll to hit.

1. Roll your weapon's damage die.
2. Subtract the target's **Armor**.
3. The target loses that much **HP**. Damage beyond HP goes to STR (chapter 9).

Melee attacks need the target Close. Ranged weapons reach Far; thrown weapons reach Near.

### Attack modifiers

- **Impaired.** Attacking from a position of weakness (bound, blinded, through cover, fighting unarmed against plate) rolls **d4** regardless of weapon.
- **Enhanced.** Attacking with a decisive edge (a clean ambush, a helpless foe, a daring stunt that paid off) rolls **d12** regardless of weapon.
- **Two weapons.** Roll both damage dice and keep the highest.
- **Several attackers, one target.** Roll all damage dice together and keep the single highest.
- **Blast.** A Blast attack affects every target in the stated area. Roll damage separately for each.

### Maneuvers **[PROPOSED]**

To shove, disarm, trip, grab, or otherwise force something other than harm, say what you are trying to do. The target makes a STR or DEX save (Warden's choice). If they fail, it works. A maneuver takes your action and deals no damage unless the Warden rules the method would.

### Morale

Opponents do not want to die. They make a **WIL save** to keep fighting:

- when the first of them falls,
- when half of them are down,
- for a lone opponent, when reduced to 0 HP.

On a failure they flee, surrender, or bargain. Player characters never roll morale.

### Retreat

Running from a fight requires a **safe destination** and a successful **DEX save**. On a failure, the character has not got away this round.

### Detachments

A large group fighting as one (a warband, a swarm) is a **detachment** with a single stat line.

- Attacks by individuals against a detachment are **impaired**, unless they are Blast.
- A detachment's attacks against individuals are **enhanced** and count as **Blast**.
- Critical damage routs or breaks a detachment. At 0 STR it is destroyed.

## 9. Harm, HP, and scars

### Hit Protection

HP is the margin by which you avoid a real wound. **A short rest restores all lost HP**, unless you are deprived.

### Exactly 0 HP: Scars

When damage reduces a character's HP to **exactly 0**, they are marked by it. Roll 1d12 on the Scars table.

### Below 0 HP: critical damage

Damage left over after HP reaches 0 is subtracted from **STR**. The character then makes a **STR save** against the new, lower score.

- **Success:** they are hurt but can keep acting.
- **Failure:** they have taken **critical damage**. They can do nothing but crawl weakly and cling to life. Without aid they **die within the hour**. Any ally can stabilize them with a few minutes of attention; they then need rest before acting again.

Some attacks have an extra effect on critical damage, listed in the attacker's entry.

### Ability score loss

- **STR 0:** death.
- **DEX 0:** paralysis.
- **WIL 0:** delirium.

A paralyzed or delirious character cannot act until the score is restored. Lost ability points return after **a week's rest with a skilled healer**.

### Scars table (d12)

| d12 | Scar | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lasting Scar** | Roll d6 for location: 1 neck, 2 hands, 3 eye, 4 chest, 5 legs, 6 ear. Roll 1d6; if it beats your max HP, that is your new max HP. |
| 2 | **Rattling Blow** | Shaken and disoriented. Say how you pull yourself together. Roll 1d6; if it beats your max HP, take it. |
| 3 | **Walloped** | Thrown down and winded. You are deprived until you rest a few hours. Then roll 1d6 and add it to your max HP. |
| 4 | **Broken Limb** | Roll d6: 1–2 leg, 3–4 arm, 5 rib, 6 skull. Once it mends, roll 2d6; if it beats your max HP, take it. |
| 5 | **Diseased** | A foul infection sets in. When you recover, roll 2d6; if it beats your max HP, take it. |
| 6 | **Reorienting Head Wound** | Roll d6: 1–2 STR, 3–4 DEX, 5–6 WIL. Roll 3d6; if it beats that score, take it. |
| 7 | **Hamstrung** | You can barely move until you get real help and rest. Afterward roll 3d6; if it beats your max DEX, take it. |
| 8 | **Deafened** | You cannot hear until you find extraordinary aid. Either way, make a WIL save; on a success raise your max WIL by 1d4. |
| 9 | **Re-brained** | Something in your mind shakes loose. Roll 3d6; if it beats your max WIL, take it. |
| 10 | **Sundered** | An appendage is torn off, crippled, or useless. The Warden says which. Make a WIL save; on a success raise your max WIL by 1d6. |
| 11 | **Mortal Wound** | You are deprived and out of action. You die in an hour unless healed. On recovery roll 2d6; that is your new max HP. |
| 12 | **Doomed** | You should have died. If you fail your next save against critical damage, you die horribly. If you pass it, roll 3d6; if it beats your max HP, take it. |

Scars are how characters toughen. Most of them leave the survivor stronger.

## 10. Deprivation, Fatigue, rest, and healing

### Deprived

A character lacking a crucial need (food, water, sleep, warmth) is **deprived**. A deprived character **cannot recover HP or ability scores**.

After more than a day deprived, the character takes **one Fatigue**, and another for each further day.

### Fatigue

Each Fatigue fills one inventory slot (chapter 5). **A full night's rest in a safe place clears all Fatigue.**

> **Dial — slow recovery.** A campaign may instead rule that a safe night clears only **one** Fatigue. This makes exhaustion, and any module that spends Fatigue, much harsher. Note the choice on the Campaign Card.

### Healing summary

| Lost | Restored by |
|---|---|
| HP | A few moments' rest and a drink |
| Fatigue | A full night's safe rest |
| Ability scores | A week's rest with a skilled healer |
| Critical damage (dying) | Stabilized by any ally within the hour, then rest |

## 11. NPCs, reactions, hirelings

### Reactions

When the party meets someone whose attitude is not already clear, roll 2d6.

| 2d6 | Reaction |
|---|---|
| 2 | Hostile |
| 3–5 | Wary |
| 6–8 | Curious |
| 9–11 | Kind |
| 12 | Helpful |

### NPCs want to live

Every NPC has self-interest and a will to survive. They remember what the characters say and do. Use morale (chapter 8).

### Hirelings

Hirelings cost **1–3 gp per day**, or a share of treasure. To make one: roll 3d6 for each ability, 1d6 for HP, give them a simple weapon (d6), and roll a few traits. Hirelings have their own morale and limits.

---

## 12. Treasure, rewards, and growth

### Treasure

Treasure is specific to where it was found and tells a story. It is highly valuable, usually bulky, and rarely useful except for its worth and prestige. Use it as a lure toward dangerous places.

**Relics** are items with a strange power. They are useful rather than valuable, usually have limited charges, and state how they recharge.

### Growth

There are no levels and no experience points in the Core Rules. Characters grow by:

- **Scars.** Most results on the Scars table raise HP or an ability.
- **Gear and relics.** What you carry is what you can do.
- **The world.** Training with a master, a patron's favor, a debt owed, a reputation earned. When a character pursues improvement in the fiction, the Warden puts a real opportunity in their path and decides what it grants.

Campaigns that want ranks and milestones use the Structured Advancement module (Part II, module 5).

---

## 13. Warden procedures

- **Information.** Describe the world usefully. Answer questions honestly. Remind players they can keep asking.
- **Difficulty.** Judge by context and realism before numbers. Impossible things fail without a roll. Sensible plans work without one. Saves cover the uncertain middle.
- **Danger.** Telegraph serious danger. The more lethal it is, the more obvious it should be. Put traps in plain sight and let players work the problem.
- **Choice.** Offer clear options when play stalls. Make sure the characters' actions leave marks on the world.
- **Preparation.** Prepare situations, not plots. Use random tables to build places and problems. Let events turn sharply when the dice or the players push them.
- **NPCs.** Give each a want and a fear. None of them wish to die.
- **Narrative focus.** What matters is what emerges at the table, not the numbers on the sheet. A knife at the throat kills regardless of armor and training.
- **Treasure.** See chapter 12.
- **Die of Fate.** See chapter 3.

---

## 14. Player principles

- **Agency.** Abilities and saves are tools, not your character's limits. Ask what your character would do, and also what you would do.
- **Teamwork.** Seek agreement before charging ahead. You achieve more together.
- **Exploration.** Questions and careful listening are worth more than any stat or item. Trust the Warden's description and ask for more.
- **Talking.** Treat NPCs as real people. Most would rather talk than fight.
- **Caution.** Fighting is a choice. Stack the odds or walk away.
- **Planning.** Scout, research, and find the way around.
- **Ambition.** Set goals. Expect nothing. Earn your reputation.

## 15. Bestiary and making threats

### Stat block format

> **Name**
> X HP, X Armor, X STR, X DEX, X WIL, attack (dX, qualities)
> - What it looks like or how it carries itself.
> - A want, tactic, or peculiarity.
> - A special effect, often triggered by critical damage.

Armor is omitted when it is 0.

### Guidelines

- **Abilities:** 3 deficient, 6 weak, 10 average, 14 noteworthy, 18 legendary.
- **HP:** 3 for ordinary creatures, 6 for hardy ones, 10 or more for serious threats. HP is skill and luck at avoiding harm, not body mass.
- **Good at avoiding hits?** Give HP. **Soaks damage?** Give Armor. **Strong?** STR. **Quick?** DEX. **Commanding or uncanny?** WIL.
- Armed attacks deal at least d6.
- Use the critical-damage effect to make a creature memorable.

### Converting from other old-school games

Give 1 HP per Hit Die. Most people have at least 4 HP. Use the source's morale score as a guide to WIL.

### Sample creatures

> **Bandit**
> 4 HP, 1 Armor, 10 STR, 11 DEX, 8 WIL, sword (d8) or bow (d6)
> - Hungry, ragged, and watching the road.
> - Wants coin, not a fight. Flees if the leader falls.

> **Wolf**
> 4 HP, 10 STR, 14 DEX, 8 WIL, bite (d6)
> - Lean and patient. Circles before closing.
> - Hunts in packs; a pack of six or more is a detachment.

> **Root Goblin**
> 4 HP, 8 STR, 14 DEX, 8 WIL, spear (d6)
> - Small, sly thieves of the deep wood.
> - Avoid a fight unless they clearly have the edge. Guard their hoard to the death.
> - Covet objects of power and will trade for them.

> **Hooded Watcher**
> 12 HP, 9 STR, 12 DEX, 14 WIL, leystaff (d8)
> - Robed cultists who draw power from old stones and lines in the earth.
> - Each has one uncanny trick (a Power or a spell, by campaign).
> - *Critical damage:* drains part of the victim's spirit (1d4 WIL damage).

> **Cobblehound**
> 12 HP, 2 Armor, 14 STR, 1 DEX, 8 WIL, bite (d10)
> - A stone guardian set to watch a tomb or treasure. It does not leave its post.
> - Cannot be reasoned with. Is fond of a good bone.

> **Wood Troll**
> 12 HP, 15 STR, 12 DEX, 7 WIL, claws and bite (d8+d8, Blast)
> - A towering, moss-hung brute.
> - Can spend its action to regain all lost HP.
> - *Critical damage:* moss and twigs sprout from the victim's wounds.

> **Frost Elf**
> 14 HP, 1 Armor, 8 STR, 13 DEX, 14 WIL, icicle dagger (d6)
> - Beautiful, long-lived, and without scruple.
> - Shrugs off most uncanny effects. Has one of its own.

> **Boggart**
> 3 HP, 4 STR, 17 DEX, 13 WIL
> - A hairy little trickster that delights in being a nuisance.
> - Loves shiny trinkets and will not sell them for coin.
> - Its true name describes its nature. Whoever knows the name commands it.

## 16. First session and sample play

### A sample character

Mara rolls 3d6 three times: **STR 8, DEX 13, WIL 10**. She is happy with the order and does not swap. She rolls 1d6 for HP: **4**.

She rolls a name (Esme Harkness), a Background (Herbalist), and traits: lanky, weathered, curly hair, precise speech, frayed clothes, cautious, nervous, considered an oddball, once defrauded. Age 2d20+10: 31.

Gear: rations, torch, 11 gp. Rolls give brigandine (1 Armor, bulky), no helmet or shield, a sword (d8), rope, a chisel, a small bell, and a bonus tool: a saw.

Slots: sword (hand), torch (hand), brigandine (2 body), rations, rope, chisel, saw in the pack. The bell is small. **8 of 10 slots used.** Two more items and Esme drops to 0 HP.

### An example of play

> **Warden:** The stair ends at a stone door, ajar. Cold air comes through. On the lintel someone has scratched three vertical lines, fresh. Beyond, your torch shows a long hall and, about twenty paces in, a shape on the floor.
>
> **Mara (Esme):** What kind of shape?
>
> **Warden:** A body, face down. Leather armor. A spear lies beside it. The flagstones between you and it look uneven. One near the body sits slightly lower than the rest.
>
> **Jo (Bevan):** Pressure plate. I don't step on anything. Can I reach the low stone with the ten-foot pole?
>
> **Warden:** Easily. You press it and a rack of darts snaps out of the left wall, crossing the hall at chest height. You're well back. No roll.
>
> **Mara:** I go to the body, staying off that stone.
>
> **Warden:** As you kneel, you hear scraping from the dark ahead. Two small figures with spears are edging along the wall toward you. They've stopped now that you've seen them. They look more curious than angry. *(The Warden rolled 2d6 for reaction: 7, Curious.)*
>
> **Jo:** I hold up the bell from Esme's pack and let it ring. "Trade?"

No save was rolled in this scene. The trap was visible, the players asked, and a sensible approach worked.

Had it come to a fight: each player makes a DEX save to act first. Esme attacks a goblin with her sword, rolls d8 and gets 5. The goblin has no Armor and 4 HP. It drops to 0 HP and loses 1 STR (8 to 7), then must make a STR save against 7. It rolls 12 and fails: critical damage, and it is out of the fight. The other goblin has seen the first casualty and makes a WIL save for morale.

## Module 1 — Origins

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adds** | A character-creation step. Replaces nothing. |
| **Use it when** | Where a character comes from should matter: species, cultures, augmentations, bloodlines. |
| **Needs** | Nothing. |
| **Sheet** | Origin name, Trait, and any Edge or Burden. |

An **Origin** is what a character *is* before they are an adventurer: ancestry, culture, homeland, upbringing, or manufacture. It sits alongside Background (what they *did*). A setting decides which kinds of Origin exist and may rename the term (lineage, tribe, model, clan).

### The parts of an Origin

| Part | Required? | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Name and description** | Yes | Two or three sentences of fiction. |
| **Trait** | Yes | One fictional permission: something this Origin can simply do, or need not roll for, in a defined circumstance. |
| **Edge** | Optional | One small numeric benefit. |
| **Burden** | Optional | One real, recurring cost. |

### Limits

- **One Trait.** It should apply in a recognizable situation, not all the time. "Climbs timber at full speed without rolling" is a Trait. "Never fails DEX saves" is not.
- **One Edge at most**, chosen from: +1 to one ability score (maximum 18); +2 inventory slots; Advantage on saves against one narrow kind of threat.
- **A strong Trait or an Edge beyond that list needs a Burden.**
- Origins never grant HP, Armor, damage, or extra actions.
- Every Origin in a setting should be built to the same budget.

### Using Origins in play

A Trait works the way a Background does. When it applies, the character can do the thing, and the Warden does not call for a save that others would need. Origins do not gate Powers or gear unless a setting says so.

Templates and worked examples are in the Workshop (Part III, chapter 2).

## Module 2 — Powers

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adds** | Innate or trained extraordinary abilities. Replaces nothing. |
| **Use it when** | Characters should *have* abilities rather than carry them: magic in the blood, mutations, psionics, cybernetics, martial disciplines, super-powers. |
| **Needs** | A cost profile (module 3). Default is Fatigue. |
| **Sheet** | A Powers list, kept apart from inventory. |

### What a Power is

A Power is a defined ability with a stated effect and a stated cost. Powers belong to the character. They **do not occupy inventory slots** and cannot be dropped, stolen, or lent, unless the setting says the Power lives in an object.

Each Power is written in the same form:

> **Name** — *Scope (Minor / Standard / Major)*
> **Effect.** What it does, in one or two sentences, with any numbers.
> **Reach.** Self, touch, Close, Near, Far, or an area.
> **Lasts.** Instant, a few rounds, ten minutes, until rest, and so on.
> **Limit.** What it cannot do, or what it requires.

### Using a Power

1. **Say what you are doing.** Using a Power takes your action.
2. **Pay the cost.** Under the default profile, take **1 Fatigue**. The cost is paid whether or not the Power works.
3. **Are you under pressure?** If you are **in danger** (in combat, mid-fall, being chased, about to be discovered) or **deprived**, make a **WIL save**.
   - **Success:** the Power works as written.
   - **Failure:** the Power does not work. Roll or choose a **Misfire**.
4. **If you are safe and unhurried**, there is no save. The Power works.

### Amplifying

With time and safety (a few minutes of calm preparation), a character may enlarge one aspect of a Power at no extra cost: roughly double its reach, area, or duration, or affect one more target. The Warden rules what is plausible. Amplifying never raises damage dice.

### Misfires

A misfire should be about as severe as the Power that caused it, never much worse. Roll 1d6, or the Warden picks the result that fits.

| d6 | Misfire |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Fizzle.** Nothing happens. The cost is still paid. |
| 2 | **Drain.** Take one additional cost (by default, 1 more Fatigue). |
| 3 | **Backlash.** You take d6 damage. Armor does not help. |
| 4 | **Stray.** The effect lands on the wrong target or place. Warden chooses. |
| 5 | **Reversal.** The effect inverts: a ward attracts, a healing touch hurts for d4. |
| 6 | **Burnout.** The Power works, weakly (half effect), but cannot be used again until you have had a night's rest. |

A setting may supply its own misfire table.

### Scope

Scope is how big a Power is. It replaces point-buy.

| Scope | Comparable to | Cost | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Minor (a knack)** | A tool anyone could carry | None. No save. | Glow like a candle; speak to one kind of animal; sense north |
| **Standard** | A good weapon or a clever piece of gear | 1 | d6–d8 damage to one target; restore 1d6 STR; a wall for a few rounds; one question answered |
| **Major** | Something no ordinary gear can do | 2, or 1 plus a built-in risk or requirement | d10–d12 damage; Blast damage; effects lasting hours; control of a creature |

Damage from Powers follows all Core combat rules: Armor applies unless the Power says otherwise, and impaired and enhanced can apply.

### Getting Powers

A setting chooses how characters come by Powers. Common arrangements:

- **Innate:** start with one or two Standard Powers from a list tied to Origin or an affinity.
- **Trained:** learn a Power from a teacher over a season of downtime.
- **Found:** an event in play awakens one (exposure, a bargain, a relic).

Without the Structured Advancement module, a character may know **at most 4 Powers**. New Powers come only from events in the fiction, never automatically.

### Themes

A setting may group Powers into themes (schools, harmonies, disciplines, power sets) and limit a character to one or two themes. This is the simplest way to keep characters distinct without classes.

### Powers and Spellbooks together

Both can exist in one campaign. Spellbooks are objects that take slots and anyone can use them. Powers are personal and take none. Both cost Fatigue by default, so a character leaning on both runs out of room quickly.

## Module 3 — Power cost profiles

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adds** | Alternatives to Fatigue as the cost of a Power. |
| **Use it when** | Fatigue does not fit the fiction of where Powers come from. |
| **Needs** | Module 2. |
| **Sheet** | Whatever the chosen profile tracks. |

Pick **one profile per source of power** in the setting. Write it on the Campaign Card. "1 cost" in module 2 means one unit of the chosen profile.

| Profile | One unit of cost is | Recovers by | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fatigue (default)** | 1 Fatigue in an inventory slot | A safe night's rest | Powers that tire the body. Ties power use to carrying capacity. |
| **Charges** | 1 charge from a pool (suggest 3) | A stated condition: a night's rest, sunlight, a power outlet | Technology, batteries, relic-like abilities |
| **Consumable** | 1 item used up (a reagent, a cell, a dose). Items take slots as normal. | Buying or finding more | Alchemy, ammunition-fed devices |
| **Strain** | 1d4 HP lost, which cannot push you below 0 HP into STR | Short rest, as normal HP | Pulp heroes, martial techniques. Cheap out of combat, dangerous in it. |
| **Exposure** | 1 mark on a corruption track (module 7) | The track's treatment | Forbidden or tainted power |
| **Time** | Ten uninterrupted minutes of ritual. Cannot be used in danger at all. | — | Ceremony, engineering, hacking |
| **Cooldown** | The Power is unavailable until a short rest | Short rest | Heroic and superheroic play |
| **None** | Nothing | — | Minor Powers always. Standard and Major only at Heroic scale (module 10). |

The pressure save in module 2 applies under every profile except Time.

## Module 4 — Spellbook compatibility

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adds** | Cairn-style magic carried as objects. |
| **Use it when** | You want magic to be loot: found, traded, stolen, and usable by anyone. |
| **Needs** | Nothing. Works with or without module 2. |
| **Sheet** | Spellbooks are inventory items. |

### Spellbooks

- A spellbook holds **one spell** and takes **one slot**.
- Spellbooks cannot be copied or created. They are found.
- **Anyone** can cast from one by holding it in **both hands** and reading aloud.
- Casting adds **1 Fatigue**.
- Given time and safety, the caster can enlarge the effect (more targets, greater scale) at no extra cost.
- Casting **in danger or while deprived** requires a **WIL save**. On a failure the Warden imposes a consequence on the scale of the spell: more Fatigue, injury, destruction of the book, or death.

### Scrolls

A scroll holds one spell, takes **no slot**, causes **no Fatigue**, and is destroyed when read.

### Relics

A relic is an object with a built-in power. Using it causes no Fatigue. It has a number of **charges** and a stated **recharge** condition.

- **Honeyclasp** (3 charges). A rusted ring. The wearer shrinks to six inches tall. *Recharge:* rest it in a thimble of royal jelly.
- **Falconer's Friend** (1 charge). A bolt-shaped wand holding Haste. *Recharge:* fire it from a crossbow and recover it.
- **Staff of Silence** (1 charge). A blackened rod. Suppresses magic within 50 ft. *Recharge:* bathe it in the light of a full moon.
- **Leycap** (1 use). A mushroom. Eating it clears one Fatigue. Make a WIL save or become addicted: deprived and unable to focus until you eat another.

The full list of 100 spells is in Appendix F.

## Module 5 — Structured advancement

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adds** | Ranks and milestones on top of Core growth. Scars still work as written. |
| **Use it when** | A long campaign wants visible progress, or Powers need a cap that rises. |
| **Needs** | Nothing. Most useful with module 2. |
| **Sheet** | Rank (1–5) and milestone boxes. |

### Ranks

Characters begin at **Rank 1** and may reach **Rank 5**. A setting names the ranks.

### Milestones

The Warden awards a **milestone** to each character who took part in a major accomplishment. Before play, the setting or the group writes **three to five milestone triggers**. Examples: complete a mission for the community; survive a place no one returns from; defeat or outwit a named enemy; recover something thought lost.

Gold never buys a milestone.

**To rise to the next Rank, earn milestones equal to that Rank's number**: 2 to reach Rank 2, 3 more to reach Rank 3, and so on. Fourteen milestones take a character from Rank 1 to Rank 5.

### What a Rank gives

On reaching a new Rank:

1. **HP.** Roll 1d6 per Rank you now hold. If the total beats your max HP, it is your new max HP.
2. **Abilities.** Roll a d20 for each ability score. If the roll is higher than the score, raise that score by 1 (maximum 18).
3. **Powers** (if module 2 is active). You may know up to **2 Powers per Rank**. Reaching a Rank raises the cap. Each new Power must still be learned in the fiction.
4. At **Rank 3** a character may open a **second theme** of Powers, if the setting uses themes.

## Module 6 — Alternate injury

| | |
|---|---|
| **Replaces** | The Scars table, or when it is rolled. |
| **Use it when** | The default tone is too harsh or too gentle. |
| **Needs** | Nothing. |
| **Sheet** | Nothing new. |

Choose at most one dial and one table.

### Dials

- **Brutal.** Roll on the Scars table at exactly 0 HP **and** whenever STR is reduced by damage. (Sylvanholt uses this.)
- **Heroic.** No Scar roll at exactly 0 HP. A failed STR save against critical damage means the character is out of the scene, not dying. Death occurs only at STR 0.
- **Lingering.** Lost ability points return at 1 per day of rest instead of all at once after a week.

### Tables

A setting may supply its own d12 table. Keep three properties of the original:

1. About half the results should leave the survivor stronger.
2. One or two results should be dire.
3. Every result should say how it is recovered from.

The Sylvanholt table (Part IV) is a worked example.

## Module 7 — Corruption and stress tracks

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adds** | A named track that fills from exposure to something harmful. |
| **Use it when** | The setting has a creeping threat: taint, radiation, madness, a curse, corporate conditioning. |
| **Needs** | Nothing. Module 3's Exposure profile feeds it. |
| **Sheet** | One row of boxes per track. |

A setting defines each track by filling in six fields:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | What specific event puts a character on the track, or adds a mark? |
| **Marks** | How many boxes before the end state? (Suggest 3.) |
| **Check** | When does the character save to avoid another mark, and with which ability? |
| **Symptoms** | What does each mark look like and do? |
| **Treatment** | What removes marks? Who or where? |
| **End state** | What happens when the track fills? Is it reversible? |

### Two shapes

- **Chronic.** Once triggered, the character saves at a set interval (each dawn, each session). Failures add marks. It continues until treated. Use for infections, curses, and slow taint.
- **Acute.** A condition that applies while exposed and for a short time after. It adds no marks itself but worsens other things (Disadvantage on a kind of save, costs that do not clear). Use for hostile zones and fields.

### Rules for end states

- The end state must be **known to the players** from the first mark.
- It should take **at least three failures** to reach.
- The setting decides whether it is reversible. If it removes a character from play, provide a route to recovery through the fiction, even a hard one.

## Module 8 — Item modification

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adds** | A downtime procedure to give an item one special quality. |
| **Use it when** | Gear should grow with the characters. |
| **Needs** | Nothing. |
| **Sheet** | Note the quality beside the item. |

A setting defines:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| **Site** | Where can it be done? (A forge, a lab, a shrine.) Sites should be rare enough to be destinations. |
| **Catalyst** | What is consumed? It should be found through play. |
| **Crafter's save** | Which ability? (Default WIL.) |
| **Qualities** | The short list of qualities available. |

### Procedure

1. Bring one ordinary item and one catalyst to a site, during downtime.
2. The lead crafter makes the save.
3. **Success:** the item gains the quality.
4. **Failure:** the catalyst is consumed, the item is unchanged, and the crafter takes 1 Fatigue.

### Limits on qualities

- **One quality per item.**
- A quality should be **situational**: it applies in a place, against a kind of foe, or on a trigger such as critical damage.
- Suitable benefits: enhanced damage in a stated circumstance; +1 to (or Advantage on) one kind of save; a rider on critical damage; immunity to one narrow hazard.
- A quality never adds flat damage, raises Armor above the cap of 3, or works everywhere.
- Qualities from a dangerous source should carry a drawback.

## Module 9 — Movement and scale cards

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adds** | Finer control over tabletop movement than Core chapter 6. |
| **Use it when** | You play with miniatures and want the table to behave a particular way. |
| **Needs** | A miniature scale from Core chapter 6. |
| **Sheet** | Write the move distance on each record. |

Core chapter 6 already lets a campaign choose Mind, Skirmish (6"), Classic (8"), or Grid. This module adds dials. Record any you use on the scale card (Appendix C).

- **Engagement.** A figure Close to an enemy who is free to act is *engaged*. To move away, it must spend its action as well as its move, or make a DEX save; on a failure it does not get away this round.
- **Charge.** A figure that moves at least half its move in a straight line into melee and attacks may be treated as enhanced if the Warden agrees the charge was decisive (downhill, into a flank). This is a reminder of the existing enhanced rule, not a new bonus.
- **Cover.** Hard cover between attacker and target makes a ranged attack impaired. Soft cover (brush, smoke) does so only beyond Near.
- **Elevation.** A clear height advantage with a ranged weapon extends Far by one move.
- **Varied movers.** Give some creatures 4", 9", or 12" moves on the Skirmish scale. Slow or encumbered characters (carrying 9 of 10 slots) move 4".
- **Scatter.** A Blast that misfires or is thrown blind lands d6 inches from its mark in a random direction.

| Play area | Suggested move | Far |
|---|---|---|
| 2 ft × 2 ft | 4" | 12" |
| 3 ft × 3 ft | 6" | 18" |
| 4 ft × 4 ft or larger | 8" | 24" |

## Module 10 — Genre and scale options

### Technology

- **Firearms.** Pistol d6. Rifle d8, bulky. Heavy weapon d10, bulky. Automatic fire and explosives are Blast.
- **Ammunition** is not counted. On a damage roll of 1, the weapon is empty or jammed and needs an action to fix.
- **Modern armor** uses the same 1–3 scale: vest 1, tactical armor 2, powered suit 3.
- **Devices** are gear. A device that does something impossible for ordinary gear is a relic (charges and a recharge condition) or a Power using the Charges profile.

### Horror

- **Stress.** Witnessing something that should not be requires a WIL save. On a failure, lose d4 WIL. At WIL 0 the character is delirious, per the Core Rules.
- WIL lost to stress returns only in safety and comfort, at 1 per day.
- Combat is unchanged. It is already frightening.

### Heroic scale

*Changes the Core assumption that characters are fragile.*

- Starting HP is **2d6**. Use the Heroic injury dial (module 6).
- Each character starts with **3 Powers**, at least one Minor.
- Use the **Cooldown** or **None** cost profile.
- **Scale.** Rate anything as Ordinary, Heroic, or Colossal. Attacks from a higher scale against a lower one are **enhanced**. Attacks from a lower scale against a higher one are **impaired**. Equal scales fight normally. A hero punching a tank and a soldier shooting a hero both follow from this one rule.

### Vehicles and mounts

A vehicle is a stat block: HP (handling and luck), Armor, STR (hull), and extra slots. Critical damage disables it. The driver's DEX is used for saves. Ramming is an attack: d8 for a cart, d12 and Blast for a truck.

## Module 11 — Cohorts and campaign scale

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adds** | Rules for followers and holdings beyond hirelings. |
| **Use it when** | Characters lead people or defend a place. |
| **Needs** | Nothing. |
| **Sheet** | A line per cohort or holding. |

- **Cohort.** A named follower who stays for loyalty rather than pay. Built like a hireling. Makes a WIL save for loyalty only when asked to do something against their own interest.
- **Warband.** Ten or more followers fight as a detachment (Core chapter 8) using the stats of a typical member with HP doubled.
- **Holding.** A settlement or base has three scores from 3 to 18: **Defense** (walls and watch), **Supply** (food and stores), **Spirit** (morale). When it is threatened, the Warden calls for a save against the relevant score. A failed save lowers that score by d4. A score at 0 means the holding falls. Characters raise scores by completing tasks in the fiction.

# PART III — MAKE-YOUR-OWN WORKSHOP

This part is for anyone with an idea and no certainty about how to make it playable. Each chapter gives a form to fill in, questions to answer, limits to respect, and examples. Blank copies of every form are in Appendix D.

---

## 1. How to customize without breaking the game

Follow the same six steps for anything you make.

1. **Concept.** One sentence of fiction. "A beaver-folk engineer who can carry more than anyone."
2. **Scope.** How big is it? Compare it to ordinary gear (chapter 8).
3. **Procedure.** When does it apply, and what happens? Prefer "this just works in situation X" to a new die roll.
4. **Cost or risk.** What does it use up, or what can go wrong?
5. **Compatibility check.** Run the five questions below.
6. **Play it, then revise.** Use it for two sessions before judging.

### The five compatibility questions

1. Does it leave **attacks hitting automatically**? Nothing should add a to-hit roll or a dodge roll.
2. Does it respect the **Armor cap of 3**?
3. Does it keep **damage on the d4–d12 ladder**, using impaired and enhanced rather than flat bonuses?
4. Does it leave **HP low**? Outside Heroic scale, nothing should hand out HP.
5. Can you explain it **in two sentences**? If not, cut it down.

If the answer to any is no, either change the design or state openly that your campaign is altering a Core assumption.

## 2. Create an Origin

*Requires module 1.*

### Form

> **Origin name:**
> **Kind:** (ancestry / culture / homeland / upbringing / manufacture)
> **Description:** (2–3 sentences)
> **Trait:** In [situation], you can [do something] without a save / at full speed / when others could not.
> **Edge (optional):** +1 [ability] / +2 slots / Advantage vs [narrow threat]
> **Burden (optional):**
> **Starting kit (optional):** a d6 table of weapon, armor, tools

### Prompts

- What can these people do that would surprise an outsider?
- Where are they most at home? The Trait probably applies there.
- What do they need, lack, or suffer that others do not? That is a Burden.
- Would every player want this Origin for the mechanics alone? If so, it is too strong.

### Limits

See module 1. In short: one Trait tied to a situation, at most one Edge from the list, a Burden if either is strong, and never HP, Armor, damage, or actions.

### Examples

> **Highlander** *(homeland)*
> Raised on thin air and steep ground.
> **Trait:** Altitude and cold that would leave others deprived do not affect you. You climb rock without a save unless the conditions are extreme.

> **Vat-Grown** *(manufacture)*
> Decanted as an adult for a job that no longer exists.
> **Trait:** You do not need sleep. You spend night watches awake and alert.
> **Edge:** +1 STR.
> **Burden:** You require a weekly dose of stabilizer (one slot per four doses) or become deprived.

> **Bavar** *(ancestry, Sylvanholt)*
> Broad, patient beaver-folk builders.
> **Trait:** You swim rapids and violent currents without a save.
> **Edge:** +1 STR and 12 inventory slots instead of 10.
> *Note: two Edges. This exceeds the standard budget and is kept as established Sylvanholt content. See `DECISIONS.md`.*

## 3. Create equipment

### Form

> **Name:**
> **Slots:** small / 1 / bulky (2)
> **Type:** weapon / armor / tool / consumable / relic
> **Numbers:** damage die, or Armor value, or uses/charges
> **Qualities:** (ranged, Blast, two-handed, and so on)
> **What it lets you do:** (one sentence)
> **Cost and availability:**

### Weapons

Choose the die by how the weapon is carried, not by how deadly it sounds.

| Carried as | Die |
|---|---|
| Improvised or unarmed | d4 |
| Light, one hand, concealable | d6 |
| A proper one-handed weapon | d8 |
| Two-handed and bulky | d10 |

Nothing wielded by a person deals d12 as its base. d12 is reserved for enhanced attacks. Ranged weapons run one step lower than melee for the same bulk (bow d6 bulky, crossbow d8 bulky). **Blast** is worth at least a die step: a Blast weapon should be d6 or d8 and be bulky, limited-use, or dangerous to its user.

### Armor

| Armor | Value |
|---|---|
| A stackable piece (shield, helmet, padding) | +1 |
| Light suit | 1, bulky |
| Medium suit | 2, bulky |
| Heavy suit | 3, bulky |

Total Armor never exceeds 3.

### Tools

A tool lets a character do something they otherwise could not, or do it without a save. It needs no numbers. "Crowbar: forces doors and lids that hands cannot."

### Consumables and relics

Give a consumable a number of uses. Give a relic charges (1–3) and a recharge condition that is an activity in the world, not a timer.

### Price guide

| Price | Kind of item |
|---|---|
| 1–5 gp | Simple tools, light weapons, basic supplies |
| 10–25 gp | Proper weapons, light armor, specialist tools |
| 30–60 gp | Heavy armor, crossbows, animals, instruments |
| 75 gp and up | Mounts, wagons, rare goods |

### Examples

> **Grapnel launcher.** 1 slot. Tool. Fires a hook and line to anything within Near. Reloading takes a turn.

> **Flare pistol.** 1 slot. Weapon, d6, ranged. Three flares (small, bundled). A target that takes critical damage is set alight.

> **Storm lantern** *(relic, 2 charges).* Releases a clap of thunder; everyone Near but the bearer is deafened for a few rounds. *Recharge:* leave it outdoors through a thunderstorm.

## 4. Create a Power

*Requires module 2.*

### Form

> **Name:**
> **Theme / source:**
> **Scope:** Minor / Standard / Major
> **Effect:**
> **Reach:** self / touch / Close / Near / Far / area of ___
> **Lasts:** instant / ___ rounds / 10 minutes / until rest
> **Limit:**
> **Cost:** (per the campaign's profile; 0 for Minor, 1 for Standard, 2 for Major)
> **Special misfire (optional):**

### Prompts

- What does it look, sound, and smell like?
- What piece of gear is it most similar to? Price it like that gear.
- What can it not do? Every Power needs a Limit.
- How would an enemy deal with it? If you cannot think of an answer, narrow it.

### Benchmarks

| Effect | Standard | Major |
|---|---|---|
| Damage, one target | d6 or d8 | d10 or d12 |
| Damage, area | d6 Blast in a small area | d8 Blast |
| Healing | Restore 1d6 STR, or remove one condition | Restore an ability fully; stabilize and wake the dying |
| Protection | +1 or +2 Armor to a few allies for a few rounds (cap 3) | Immunity to one kind of harm for a scene |
| Control | A save or be hindered for a round or two | A save or be commanded briefly |
| Movement | One extraordinary move (leap, climb, glide) | Flight or teleportation within Far |
| Information | One clear answer or sense | Sustained perception at a distance |
| Shaping | An obstacle or bridge about 10–20 ft | A structure, or one lasting hours |

A Power that only does what a 10 gp tool does should be **Minor**.

### Examples

> **Soothing Hum** — *Standard (Sylvanholt, Hearth)*
> **Effect.** Restores 1d6 STR to a living creature. **Reach.** Touch. **Lasts.** Instant. **Limit.** Does not remove corruption. **Cost.** 1 Fatigue.

> **Reflex Splice** — *Standard (science-fiction augment, Charges profile)*
> **Effect.** Act first this round regardless of saves, and your next DEX save has Advantage. **Reach.** Self. **Lasts.** One round. **Limit.** Needs the implant; an EMP disables it. **Cost.** 1 charge of 3; recharges at any power outlet in ten minutes.

> **Kinetic Slam** — *Major (superheroic, Cooldown profile)*
> **Effect.** d8 Blast to everything Close to you, and each target makes a STR save or is thrown to Near. **Reach.** Self, area. **Lasts.** Instant. **Limit.** You must be standing on solid ground. It damages the ground. **Cost.** Unavailable until a short rest.

> **Trailsense** — *Minor (ordinary scout)*
> **Effect.** You always know which way is north and how long until dark. **Cost.** None.

## 5. Create an enemy or NPC

### Form

> **Name**
> ___ HP, ___ Armor, ___ STR, ___ DEX, ___ WIL, attack (d___, qualities)
> - *Appearance or manner:*
> - *Want, tactic, or peculiarity:*
> - *Critical damage or special effect:*

### Steps

1. **Start from the fiction.** What is it, what does it want, and what does it do when frightened?
2. **Set abilities** from the 3 / 6 / 10 / 14 / 18 scale. Leave most at 10.
3. **Set HP:** 3 ordinary, 6 hardy, 10+ a serious threat.
4. **Armor** only if it is armored or unusually hard to hurt.
5. **Attack die** from the weapon table. Blast if it hits several at once.
6. **Add one memorable thing.** A critical-damage effect is the easiest.
7. **Decide how it is telegraphed.** What do the characters notice before it strikes?

### Danger check

Compare the creature's attack to a starting character with about 3 HP and 10 STR.

| Attack | Threat |
|---|---|
| d6 | Dangerous in numbers |
| d8 | Can drop a character in two rounds |
| d10–d12 or Blast | Can kill in one exchange. Telegraph it heavily. |

### Example

> **Signal-Jacker**
> 5 HP, 1 Armor, 9 STR, 13 DEX, 12 WIL, shock baton (d6)
> - A scavenger in a coat of stolen antennae.
> - Wants working devices. Would rather steal than fight.
> - *Critical damage:* the victim's powered gear shorts out until repaired.

## 6. Create a condition, hazard, or track

### Hazard form

> **Name:**
> **Warning:** what the characters see, hear, or smell before it matters
> **Trigger:** what sets it off
> **Effect:** damage die, or save and consequence
> **Ways around it:** at least two

A hazard with no warning and one solution is a punishment, not a puzzle.

### Condition form

> **Name:**
> **Caused by:**
> **Effect:** one sentence. Prefer "cannot do X" or "X is impaired" to a number.
> **Ends when:**

The Core Rules have only one named condition, **deprived**. Add others sparingly.

### Track form

*Requires module 7.* Fill in Trigger, Marks, Check, Symptoms, Treatment, End state.

> **Example — Rad-Sickness** *(chronic)*
> **Trigger:** critical damage from an irradiated creature, or a watch spent in a hot zone unprotected.
> **Marks:** 3. **Check:** STR save each dawn.
> **Symptoms:** 1 nausea, cannot benefit from rations today; 2 lose 1d4 STR; 3 hair and teeth loosen, deprived.
> **Treatment:** anti-rad course (rare), or a week in a clean shelter per mark.
> **End state:** at a fourth failure, STR damage becomes permanent. Reversible only by advanced medicine.

## 7. Create a setting pack

A setting pack is a document that makes these declarations, in this order:

1. **Pitch.** Three sentences: the world, the threat, what characters do.
2. **Campaign Card.** Modules on, scale, dials.
3. **Renames.** Any terms the setting calls something else (Warden, Origin, Power, gold).
4. **Character creation.** Any changed or added steps, and replacement tables for names, backgrounds, and gear.
5. **Origins** (if module 1).
6. **Powers** (if module 2): source, themes, cost profile, lists, any special misfires.
7. **Advancement** (if module 5): rank names and milestone triggers.
8. **Tracks** (if module 7).
9. **Gear and prices.**
10. **Item modification** (if module 8): sites, catalysts, qualities.
11. **Bestiary.**
12. **Campaign frame.** What a typical session's objective looks like.
13. **Setting rules.** Any exception to the Core Rules, listed in one place and labeled.
14. **Glossary.**

A pack is complete when a player who has read the Core Rules and the pack can make a character and play without any other document.

## 8. Balance without point-buy

Waymark has no build points. Use these five checks.

1. **The gear test.** What ordinary item does this resemble? It should cost about what carrying that item costs: a slot, a hand, some coin, some risk.
2. **The frequency test.** How often will it come up? Something that applies every round must be weak. Something that applies once a session can be strong.
3. **The counterplay test.** Name two ways an opponent or situation can negate it.
4. **The spotlight test.** Does it do another character's job better than they can? If so, narrow it.
5. **The "everyone takes it" test.** If no player would pass it up, it is too good. If no player would take it, it is too weak or too dull.

When in doubt, make it **narrower**, not weaker. A strong effect in a specific situation is more fun and safer than a mild effect that is always on.

A player wants a "lightning spear." Run the loop:

1. *Concept:* a spear that stores a storm.
2. *Scope:* more than ordinary gear, so it is a relic.
3. *Procedure:* a normal spear (d8). Spend a charge to make one attack Blast against everything Close to the target.
4. *Cost:* 2 charges. Recharges when it is planted in open ground during a thunderstorm.
5. *Check:* still auto-hit, no Armor change, d8 on the ladder, two sentences. Passes.

# PART IV — SETTING PACK

# SYLVANHOLT: THE FIRST SONG

*A biotechnological fantasy of small folk, a living forest, and a song that is going quiet.*

This pack plus the Core Rules is everything needed to play in Sylvanholt. Where this pack differs from the Core Rules, the difference is listed under **Setting rules** (section 3).

---

## 1. The world

Before there was green, there was gray. Sylvanholt, the forest continent of the world of Aelinra, was born from silence when the **Golden Stag** breathed the **First Song** into its soil. From that resonance grew the **First Oak**, a living amplifier that anchored the Song in the deep roots of the earth. From its golden acorns came six tribes to tend the continent: the Skwirls of the high canopy, the scholarly Mycen burrowers, the grounded Veylen seers, the sleek Shryg scouts, the massive Bavar builders, and the cunning Ratkyn survivors.

Magic here is not formula. It is the tuning of one's own body to the First Song. Those who align with its golden resonance channel **Ardenfire**, in five harmonies: Hearth (healing), Shield (warding), Blaze (heat and flame), Root (living masonry), and Wild (communion with beasts). A practitioner is called a **Note**.

The harmony is under siege. **Noctis**, an ancient figure of pride, twisted the relics the Stag left behind to forge **Noctimancy**, a sickly violet dissonance that corrupts, decays, and controls.

**The campaign era.** It is one or two generations since the Stag departed Aelinra and the **Great Root Severing** weakened the Song. Nothing is a ruin yet. The Great Holds, such as the river-fortress of **Timberdeep**, are being built now. The characters are not treasure hunters. They are partisans, scouts, and defenders holding the line against Noctis and his general **Malakar**: destroying **Riftbound Anchors**, attuning gear at **Resonant Anvils**, and protecting living communities before the Song fades.

## 3. Setting rules

These are the places Sylvanholt departs from the Core Rules.

1. **Tribes.** Every character belongs to one of six Tribes (section 5). Core names and gear tables are replaced by the Tribe tables.
2. **Scars.** Roll on the Sylvanholt Scars table at exactly 0 HP **and** whenever damage reduces STR.
3. **Dying.** A character who fails the STR save against critical damage falls unconscious and dies within the hour unless treated by a healer or stabilized with a Hearthnote.
4. **Fatigue recovery.** A full night's rest in a safe sanctuary clears **1 Fatigue**. (Restorative Warmth raises this to 2.)
5. **Healing.** The Hearthnote *Soothing Hum* restores STR immediately, an exception to the Core week of rest.
6. **Sources of Fatigue.** Channeling a Harmonic Ability, traveling without rations, and certain Noctimantic attacks all add Fatigue.
7. **Noctimancy is not for players.** Characters cannot choose Noctimantic Dissonances.
8. **Advantage and Disadvantage** are used as defined in Core chapter 3.

---

## 4. Character creation

1. **Abilities.** Roll 3d6 for STR, DEX, WIL in order. You may swap any two.
2. **HP.** Roll 1d6.
3. **Tribe.** Choose one. Apply its +1 ability bonus (maximum 18) and note its Trait.
4. **Harmony affinity.** Choose a Primary Affinity: **Hearth, Shield, Blaze, Root, or Wild**.
   - An **awakened** character is a Note and starts with **2 Harmonic Abilities** from that harmony.
   - A character from a non-practitioner background keeps the affinity **latent**. They know no abilities until a narrative event awakens them, at which point they learn one.
5. **Starting logistics.** 3 days of rations (1 slot), a light source (1 slot), and 3d6 gold pieces.
6. **Starting equipment.** Roll 1d6 on your Tribe's table.
7. **Attunement Depth.** All characters begin at **Tier 1**.
8. Name your character and describe them. The Core trait tables may be used; read "skin" as fur or coat.

## 7. The First Song: Harmonic Abilities

Harmonic Abilities are Powers (module 2) using the Fatigue profile. The full procedure is restated here.

- **Known abilities** are written on the sheet and take **no inventory slots**.
- **Channeling** takes your action and adds **1 Fatigue** to an inventory slot.
- **In safety**, with time to align your breathing, you may amplify the scale or duration of an ability at no extra cost.
- **In danger or while deprived**, channeling requires a **WIL save**.
  - **Success:** the ability works as intended.
  - **Failure:** a **misfire**. The Warden chooses an inverse or harmful result on the scale of the ability: physical harm to the Note, a burst of extra Fatigue, or immediate unconsciousness. The Core misfire table (module 2) may be used.

### The five Ardenfire harmonies

Each ability below is Standard in scope and costs 1 Fatigue.

#### 1. Hearthnotes (Healing & Purification)
* **Soothing Hum:** Restores **1d6 STR** to a touched living creature.
* **Mend the Severed:** Knits simple physical splits, cracked wood, or snapped handles back into structural integrity.
* **Purifying Breath:** Exhales a fragrant vapor clearing non-Noctimantic poisons, mundane infections, or blindness from a target within reach.
* **Restorative Warmth:** Envelops a resting party in golden light; allows clearing **2 Fatigue** slots instead of 1 during a safe night's rest.

#### 2. Shieldnotes (Warding & Density)
* **Hardened Grain:** Aligns an object's density. Target behaves as twice as thick and twice as heavy against physical blows for 10 minutes.
* **Radiant Dome:** Drives staff/paws into floor. A shimmering dome of amber light extends in a 5ft radius, granting **+2 Armor** to allies inside against external physical projectiles for 3 rounds.
* **Resonant Ward:** Traces a golden circle. Selected creature type cannot cross the boundary unless passing a WIL save.
* **Anchor the Pylon:** Infuses a building or bridge with an anchor, rendering it immune to structural collapse from external pressure for 1 hour.

#### 3. Blazenotes (Kinetic Heat & Flare)
* **Blaze Flare:** Fires a concentrated line of heat dealing **1d8 damage** to a target within 30ft. *(Azure variant deals 1d12 and ignores Noctimantic Armor).*
* **Searing Crescent:** Releasing a horizontal arc of golden flame across a 15ft width, dealing **1d6 damage to all targets** in the zone.
* **Ember Sentinel:** Floating orb of gold light hovers above shoulder. Illuminates within 50ft; can detonate on command for **1d6 Blast damage** within 10ft.
* **Ignite Sap:** Boils sap in a wooden weapon/tool within sight. Deals **1d6 ongoing damage** per round to anyone holding it until dropped or extinguished.

#### 4. Rootnotes (Organic Masonry)
* **Thorn Whip:** Thorn-studded vine erupts from soil/bark, lashing a target within 20ft for **1d6 damage** and binding limbs on a failed DEX save.
* **Listening Grove:** Pressing palm to a living tree trunk allows hearing any movement or whisper traveling through root networks within 500ft for 1 minute.
* **Brambleshield:** Wall of dense briers surges from earth (10ft long, 5ft high). Provides cover and inflicts **1d6 damage** to anyone forcing through it.
* **Unfurl the Scroll:** Nearby roots and branches move slowly to form ladders, stairs, or bridges across gaps up to 20ft wide.

#### 5. Wildnotes (Animal Alignment)
* **Beast-Tongue:** Speak with and understand mundane forest fauna (birds, beetles, small critters) for 10 minutes.
* **Soothe the Pack:** Waves of calming amber light wash over aggressive, non-corrupted beasts. They will not attack unless provoked directly.
* **Thornrunner's Accord:** Touching a wild animal or free Thornrunner while passing a WIL save causes it to assist in a single task (tracking, bearing loads, navigating hazards) before departing.
* **Mote Swarm:** Summon specialized fireflies that pulse in unison with your heartbeat to blind opponents, illuminate caverns, or reveal hidden tripwires.

## 8. Attunement Depth

Characters do not earn experience or levels. Progress is measured by **Attunement Depth**, the Sylvanholt form of Structured Advancement (module 5).

| Tier | Name | Harmonic Abilities |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Latent / Awakened | Up to 2, Primary Affinity only |
| 2 | Attuned Practitioner | Up to 4, Primary Affinity only |
| 3 | Master Note | Up to 6. May begin discovering a **Secondary Affinity** |
| 4 | Full Chord Resonance | May master abilities across Primary and Secondary Affinities |
| 5 | Echo of the Stag | Legendary resonance. The Warden and player define what this means. |

### Rising in Tier **[PROPOSED]**

The legacy rules name what causes growth but give no procedure. This draft uses module 5's milestones. A character earns one milestone for each of the following they take part in:

- purging a Riftbound Anchor;
- discovering an ancient grove or sanctuary;
- surviving an encounter with a named servant of Noctis;
- attuning an item at a Resonant Anvil for the first time;
- saving a living community from destruction.

Milestones needed: 2 to reach Tier 2, then 3, 4, and 5 for each Tier after. On reaching a Tier, apply the HP and ability rolls from module 5. New abilities must be learned in the fiction: from a teacher, a grove, or a moment of resonance.

## 9. Noctimantic contamination

Noctimancy has five Dissonances: **Grave, Rift, Blight, Wither, and Veil**. It distorts and consumes the Song. Two tracks (module 7) represent what it does to the characters.

### Blight-Rot (chronic)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Taking critical damage (STR loss) from a Blightbound creature or a corrupted weapon. |
| **Check** | A WIL save at the start of each in-game day. |
| **Marks** | 3. Each failure spreads shadow along the veins. |
| **Treatment** | Reaching a high-resonance Sanctuary, or the care of a Hearthnote healer (by tradition, a Mycen). Treatment clears all marks. |
| **End state** | At 3 failures, **1 Tier of Attunement Depth is permanently burned away** and the marks reset. If the rot is still untreated, the checks continue. |

### Rift-Discord (acute)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Being near an active Riftbound Anchor, caught in a Dissonance explosion, or struck by certain enemy abilities. |
| **Effect** | All WIL saves to channel Harmonic Abilities are made with **Disadvantage**. Misfires are catastrophic: the Warden chooses the worst fitting result. |
| **Dissonant Fatigue** | Any Fatigue gained from channeling while under Discord is **Dissonant**. It fills a slot like ordinary Fatigue but is not cleared by rest. It must be purged at a Sanctuary. |
| **Ends** | When the character has left the source and taken a short rest. **[PROPOSED]** |

### Tier 0: the final fall

If Blight-Rot burns a character down to **Tier 0**, their spirit and body give way. They become an **Unsung** NPC under the Warden's control, lost to the party and now a threat, unless the group undertakes a specific story to bring them back.

## 10. Resonant Anvils

Gear taken from ordinary battles is **Unsung** (silent). The Stag left **Resonant Anvils** scattered across the continent. At one, during downtime, an item can be tuned toward the Song or toward the dissonance.

```
                UNSUNG ITEM
               /           \
    + Songstone or          + Violet Ore
      Amber Sap Crystal       shard
    + WIL save passed       + WIL save passed
             |                      |
      ATTUNED                 CORRUPTED
   (warm gold, amber)     (oily violet, smoke)
```

### Procedure

1. Place one Unsung weapon, shield, armor, or tool in the basin of the Anvil.
2. Consume one chunk of **Songstone** or **Amber Sap Crystal** to attune, or one shard of **Violet Ore** to corrupt.
3. The lead crafter makes a **WIL save**.
   - **Success:** the item transforms.
   - **Failure:** the frequency fractures. The catalyst is consumed, the item stays Unsung, and the crafter takes 1 Fatigue.

### Item qualities

| Item | Quality |
|---|---|
| **Attuned weapon** | *Resonant.* Attacks are enhanced (d12) while the wielder stands in a Sanctuary or high-resonance grove. Flashes warm gold on impact. |
| **Attuned armor or shield** | +1 to WIL saves against illusion, fear, and Noctimantic delirium while worn. |
| **Corrupted weapon** | When it deals critical damage, the target contracts **Blight-Rot**. |
| **Corrupted armor or shield** | The wearer is immune to cold and Grave chills, but their Armor counts as 1 lower against Blazenotes and other Ardenfire. |

## 11. Running Sylvanholt

### The partisan loop

Build sessions around tactical objectives: purge a Riftbound Anchor, protect a settlement, reach a Resonant Anvil, escort civilians out of a contested sector, scout a Hold's supply line. The characters belong to a cause and a community. Treasure is a means, not the point.

### Voice

Use the oaths of the world: *"Stag's mercy." "By the Roots." "The Song endures." "Dissonant filth."*

### Principles

All Core Warden and player principles apply. In particular: telegraph danger, put context before arithmetic, and remember that combat hits automatically and the wilds are deep.

## A. One-page Core Rules reference

**Saves.** Roll d20 equal to or under STR, DEX, or WIL. 1 always succeeds, 20 always fails. Only roll when the outcome is uncertain and risky.
**Advantage / Disadvantage.** Roll 2d20, keep lower / higher.
**Die of Fate.** d6: 4+ good for the party, 3− bad.

**Round.** Move once + one action. Give up the action to move again.
**Bands.** Close (reach) · Near (one move) · Far (three moves) · Distant.
**Scale.** Mind / Skirmish 6" / Classic 8" / Grid 6 squares. 1" = 5 ft.

**Combat start.** Warden telegraphs. Surprised side goes last. Otherwise DEX save to act before the opposition.
**Attack.** Always hits. Damage die − Armor = HP lost. Excess goes to STR.
**Impaired** d4. **Enhanced** d12. **Two weapons / several attackers:** keep highest die. **Blast:** every target in the area, rolled separately.
**Armor** adds up, maximum 3.
**Maneuver.** Target makes a STR or DEX save or it works. No damage.
**Morale.** Foes make a WIL save at first casualty and at half strength. Lone foes at 0 HP.
**Retreat.** Safe destination + DEX save.

**Exactly 0 HP.** Roll d12 on the Scars table.
**Below 0 HP.** Excess reduces STR, then STR save. Fail = critical damage: helpless, dead within the hour without aid.
**STR 0** dead · **DEX 0** paralyzed · **WIL 0** delirious.

**Inventory.** 10 slots (2 hands, 2 body, 6 pack). Bulky = 2. All slots full = 0 HP.
**Fatigue.** Fills one slot each. A safe night's rest clears it.
**Deprived.** Cannot recover HP or abilities. One Fatigue per day after the first.
**Recovery.** HP: a short rest. Abilities: a week with a healer.

**Reaction (2d6).** 2 hostile · 3–5 wary · 6–8 curious · 9–11 kind · 12 helpful.
**Event (d6).** 1 encounter · 2 sign · 3 environment · 4 loss · 5 strain · 6 quiet.

**Powers (module 2).** Action + cost (1 Fatigue). In danger or deprived: WIL save or misfire.

27. **Elasticity:** Your body can stretch up to 10ft.
28. **Elemental Wall:** A straight wall of ice or fire 50ft long and 10ft high rises from the ground.
29. **Filch:** A visible item teleports to your hands.
30. **Flare:** A bright ball of energy fires a trail of light into the sky, revealing your location to friend or foe.
31. **Fog Cloud:** A dense fog spreads out from you.
32. **Frenzy:** A nearby creature erupts in a frenzy of violence.
33. **Gate:** A portal to a random plane opens.
34. **Gravity Shift:** You can change the direction of gravity, but only for yourself.
35. **Greed:** A creature develops the overwhelming urge to possess a visible item of your choice.
36. **Haste:** Your movement speed is tripled.
37. **Hatred:** A creature develops a deep hatred of another creature or group and wishes to destroy them.
38. **Hear Whispers:** You can hear faint sounds clearly.
39. **Hover:** An object hovers, frictionless, 2ft above the ground. It can hold up to one humanoid.
40. **Hypnotize:** A creature enters a trance and will truthfully answer one yes or no question you ask it.
41. **Icy Touch:** A thick ice layer spreads across a touched surface, up to 10ft in radius.
42. **Identify Owner:** Letters appear over the object you touch, spelling out the name of the object's owners, if there are any.
43. **Illuminate:** A floating light moves as you command.
44. **Invisible Tether:** Two objects within 10ft of each other cannot be moved more than 10ft apart.
45. **Knock:** A nearby mundane or magical lock unlocks - loudly.
46. **Leap:** You jump up to 10ft high, once.
47. **Liquid Air:** The air around you becomes swimmable.
48. **Magic Dampener:** All nearby magical effects have their effectiveness halved.
49. **Manse:** A sturdy, furnished cottage appears for 12 hours. You can permit and forbid entry to it at will.
50. **Marble Craze:** Your pockets are full of marbles, and will refill every 30 seconds.
51. **Masquerade:** A character's appearance and voice becomes identical to those of a character you touch.
52. **Miniaturize:** A creature you touch is shrunk down to the size of a mouse.
53. **Mirror Image:** An illusory duplicate of yourself appears and is under your control.
54. **Mirrorwalk:** A mirror becomes a gateway to another mirror that you looked into today.
55. **Multiarm:** You temporarily gain an extra arm.
56. **Night Sphere:** A 50ft wide sphere of darkness displaying the night sky appears before you.
57. **Objectify:** You become any inanimate object between the size of a grand piano and an apple.
58. **Ooze Form:** You become a living jelly.
59. **Pacify:** A creature near you has an aversion to violence.
60. **Phobia:** A nearby creature becomes terrified of an object of your choice.
61. **Pit:** A pit 10ft wide and 10ft deep opens in the ground.
62. **Primal Surge:** A creature rapidly evolves into a future version of its species.
63. **Push/Pull:** An object of any size is pulled directly towards you or pushed directly away from you with the strength of one man.
64. **Raise Dead:** A skeleton rises from the ground to serve you. They are incredibly stupid and can only obey simple orders.
65. **Raise Spirit:** The spirit of a nearby corpse manifests and will answer 1 question.
66. **Read Mind:** You can hear the surface thoughts of nearby creatures.
67. **Repel:** Two objects are strongly magnetically repelled from each other within 10 feet.
68. **Scry:** You can see through the eyes of a creature you touched earlier today.
69. **Sculpt Elements:** Inanimate material behaves like clay in your hands.
70. **Sense:** Choose one kind of object (key, gold, arrow, jug, etc.). You can sense the nearest example.
71. **Shield:** A creature you touch is protected from mundane attacks for one minute.
72. **Shroud:** A creature you touch is invisible until they move.
73. **Shuffle:** Two creatures you can see instantly switch places.
74. **Sleep:** A creature you can see falls into a light sleep.
75. **Slick:** Every surface in a 30ft radius becomes extremely slippery.
76. **Smoke Form:** Your body becomes a living smoke that you can control.
77. **Sniff:** You can smell even the faintest traces of scents.
78. **Snuff:** The source of any mundane light you can see is instantly snuffed out.
79. **Sort:** Inanimate items sort themselves according to categories you set.
80. **Spectacle:** A clearly false but impressive illusion of your choice appears, under your control. It may be up to the size of a palace and has full motion and sound.
81. **Spellsaw:** A whirling blade flies from your chest, clearing any plant material in its way. It is otherwise harmless.
82. **Spider Climb:** You can climb surfaces like a spider.
83. **Summon Cube:** Once per second you may summon or banish a 3-foot-wide cube of earth. New cubes must be affixed to the earth or to other cubes.
84. **Swarm:** You become a swarm of crows, rats, or piranhas. You can only be harmed by blast attacks.
85. **Telekinesis:** You may mentally move 1 item under 60lbs.
86. **Telepathy:** Two creatures can hear each other's thoughts, no matter how far apart.
87. **Teleport:** An object or person you can see is transported from one place to another in a 50ft radius.
88. **Target Lure:** An object you touch becomes the target of any nearby spell.
89. **Thicket:** A thicket of trees and dense brush up to 50ft wide suddenly sprouts up.
90. **Summon Idol:** A carved stone statue the size of a mule rises from the ground.
91. **Time Control:** Time in a 50ft bubble slows down or increases by 10% for 30 seconds.
92. **True Sight:** You see through all nearby illusions.
93. **Upwell:** A spring of seawater appears.
94. **Vision:** You completely control what a creature sees.
95. **Visual Illusion:** A silent, immobile, room-sized illusion of your choice appears.
96. **Ward:** A silver circle 50ft across appears on the ground. Choose one species that cannot cross it.
97. **Web:** Your wrists shoot thick webbing.
98. **Widget:** A primitive version of a drawn tool or item appears before you and disappears after a short time.
99. **Wizard Mark:** Your finger can shoot a stream of ulfire-colored paint. This paint is only visible to you and can be seen at any distance, even through solid objects.
100. **X-Ray Vision:** You can see through walls, dirt, clothing, etc.
