# WAYMARK

**A rules-light adventure game for any world.** Draft 0.2.

One person is the **Warden**, who describes the world and plays everyone in it. Everyone else plays one character. You need a set of dice (d4 to d20), paper, and pencils.

The book has two halves. The **Core** is the whole game: learn it and you can play. The **Modules** are optional. Switch on the ones your world needs and ignore the rest.

*Based on Cairn by Yochai Gal (CC BY-SA 4.0).*

# THE CORE

## How the game works

- **Fiction first.** The Warden describes a situation. Players ask questions and say what their characters do. If it is impossible, it fails. If it is sensible, it works. Dice come out only when the outcome is uncertain and failure would cost something.
- **No classes.** A character is defined by what they carry, what they have been through, and any abilities they have.
- **Flesh is fragile.** Fights are fast and dangerous. Avoiding, outsmarting, or talking is usually wiser than attacking.
- **Danger is shown.** The Warden makes threats visible before they strike, so that players can make real choices.

## Ability scores

| Score | Used for |
|---|---|
| **Strength (STR)** | Muscle, endurance, toughness. Lifting, forcing, resisting poison, surviving wounds. |
| **Dexterity (DEX)** | Agility, reflexes, stealth, balance. Dodging, sneaking, acting first. |
| **Willpower (WIL)** | Nerve, focus, force of personality. Persuading, resisting fear, using Powers. |

10 is average. 14 is impressive. 18 is the most a person can have.

## Saves

When a character does something risky, the Warden names an ability and the player rolls a **d20**.

- **Equal to or under the score:** success.
- **Over the score:** failure.
- A **1** always succeeds. A **20** always fails.

**Advantage:** roll two d20 and keep the lower. **Disadvantage:** roll two and keep the higher.

## Making a character

1. **Abilities.** Roll 3d6 for STR, then DEX, then WIL. You may swap any two.
2. **Hit Protection.** Roll 1d6. This is your HP.
3. **Who you are.** Give your character a name and one sentence about what they did before this. That past is what they know: a former smith can judge a blade without a roll.
4. **Gear.** You start with three days of rations, a light source, and 3d6 coins. Then either roll on your world's starting kit table, or choose one weapon (d6 or d8), one piece of armor worth 1 Armor, and two tools.

If your game uses Modules, they add steps here: choose an Origin, choose an Affinity and starting Powers.

## Inventory and Fatigue

A character has **10 slots**: 2 hands, 2 on the body, 6 in a pack.

- Most items take **1 slot**. **Bulky** items take **2**. Small items bundle together.
- **If every slot is full, you drop to 0 HP** from exhaustion and stay there until you free one. You cannot carry more than your slots.
- **Fatigue** is tiredness that takes up space. Each Fatigue fills one slot. Going without food or sleep for a day gives you one. So does using a Power.
- **A full night's rest somewhere safe clears 1 Fatigue.**

## Combat

**Who goes first.** The Warden says what the enemies are about to do. Each player makes a **DEX save**. Succeed and you act before the enemies. Fail and you act after them.

**On your turn** you can move and do one thing: attack, use an item, use a Power, help someone.

**Attacks always hit.** Roll your weapon's damage die, subtract the target's **Armor**, and the target loses that much HP.

| Situation | Damage |
|---|---|
| Unarmed | d4 |
| **Impaired**: bound, blind, badly positioned, broken tools | d4, whatever the weapon |
| **Enhanced**: ambush, high ground, helpless target | d12, whatever the weapon |
| Two weapons | Roll both, keep the higher |
| Several attackers on one target | Roll all, keep the single highest |
| **Blast** | Hits everyone in the area. Roll for each. |

**Armor** is subtracted from every damage roll. A shield or helmet adds +1. Armor never totals more than **3**.

**Getting away.** Fleeing a fight takes somewhere to flee to and a DEX save.

**Enemies want to live.** Most will run, bargain, or surrender when a fight turns against them. The Warden decides when.

## Getting hurt

**HP is not health.** It is luck, poise, and grit: your ability to avoid a real wound. A few minutes' rest and a drink of water restores all of it.

**Exactly 0 HP.** You are marked. Roll on the Scars table.

**Past 0 HP.** Leftover damage comes off your **STR**. Roll on the Scars table, then make a **STR save** against your new score.

- **Succeed:** you are wounded but still up.
- **Fail:** you fall unconscious and die within the hour unless someone treats you.

**Lost ability points** come back with a week of rest and care.

**A score at 0:** STR 0 is death. DEX 0 is paralysis. WIL 0 is delirium.

**Deprived.** A character without food, water, sleep, or warmth is deprived. They cannot recover HP or ability points until the need is met.

### Scars (d12)

| d12 | Scar | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lasting Scar** | A mark you will always carry. Roll d6: 1 neck, 2 hands, 3 eye, 4 chest, 5 legs, 6 ear. Roll 1d6; if higher than your max HP, that is your new max. |
| 2 | **Rattling Blow** | Shaken. Describe how you refocus. Roll 1d6; if higher than max HP, take it. |
| 3 | **Walloped** | Flattened. Deprived until you rest a few hours. Then add 1d6 to max HP. |
| 4 | **Broken Limb** | Roll d6: 1–2 leg, 3–4 arm, 5 rib, 6 skull. Useless for a week. Once mended, roll 2d6; if higher than max HP, take it. |
| 5 | **Diseased** | The wound festers. No HP from short rests until cured. Then roll 2d6; if higher than max HP, take it. |
| 6 | **Head Wound** | Roll d6: 1–2 STR, 3–4 DEX, 5–6 WIL. Roll 3d6; if higher than that score, take it. |
| 7 | **Hamstrung** | You can only limp until treated and rested a week. Then roll 3d6; if higher than DEX, take it. |
| 8 | **Deafened** | You cannot hear. Make a WIL save; succeed and raise max WIL by 1d4. |
| 9 | **Re-brained** | Something shakes loose. Roll 3d6; if higher than WIL, take it. |
| 10 | **Sundered** | You lose a finger, ear, eye, or similar. The Warden says which. Make a WIL save; succeed and raise max WIL by 1d6. |
| 11 | **Mortal Wound** | Deprived and out of action. Dead in an hour unless treated. On recovery, roll 2d6 for a new max HP. |
| 12 | **Doomed** | Death brushed past. Your next STR save against a wound fails automatically. Survive this encounter and make a STR save; succeed and roll 3d6 for a new max HP. |

Scars are how characters grow tougher.

## Gear

| Weapon | Damage | Slots | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light: knife, club, staff, sickle | d6 | 1 | 5 |
| Standard: sword, spear, axe, mace | d8 | 1 | 10 |
| Heavy: great hammer, greataxe, polearm | d10 | 2 | 20 |
| Sling or thrown | d4 | 1 | 5 |
| Short bow, hand crossbow | d6 | 2 | 20 |
| Longbow, crossbow | d8 | 2 | 30 |

| Armor | Armor | Slots | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shield or helmet | +1 | 1 | 10 |
| Light: padded, leather | 1 | 1 | 15 |
| Medium: mail, scale | 2 | 2 | 40 |
| Heavy: plate | 3 | 2 | 60 |

**Everything else** is a tool that lets you do something you otherwise could not: rope, a crowbar, lockpicks, a healer's kit, a lantern. Simple tools cost 1–5 coins, specialist tools 10–25. Three days of rations cost 10.

A world can rename and reprice any of this. A laser pistol is a d6 weapon. A flak vest is light armor.

## Running the game

**For the Warden**

- **Show the danger.** Put hazards in plain sight and let players solve them by thinking.
- **Context before math.** No roll for the impossible or the obvious.
- **Answer questions honestly.** Players never roll just to learn what their characters can see.
- **Give them something to do.** Build sessions around a clear objective.

**For players**

- **Your sheet is a toolbox, not a fence.** Ask what you would do in the situation.
- **Fight last.** Ambush, stack the odds, or negotiate.
- **Ask questions.** Details about the scene are worth more than any stat.

**Creatures** are written on one line, with a note or two:

> **Name.** HP, Armor, STR, DEX, WIL, attack (die).
> *What it is like. What it wants. What happens when it wounds someone badly.*

3 HP is ordinary, 6 is tough, 10 or more is a serious threat. Leave most scores at 10.

# THE MODULES

Each module is independent unless it says otherwise. A world lists which ones it uses.

## Module: Origins

*Use when where a character comes from should matter: species, cultures, bloodlines, builds.*

An Origin gives a character two things:

- **+1 to one ability score** (to a maximum of 18).
- **One Trait:** something this kind of person can simply do. It should apply in a particular situation, not all the time.

A stronger Trait should come with a drawback.

> *Example.* **Canopy-born.** +1 DEX. You climb trees at full speed without a save, and can glide down from any height.
>
> *Example.* **Quickblood.** +1 DEX. You always act first when an ambush is sprung. You must eat double rations or become deprived.

**The world supplies:** a list of Origins, and optionally a starting kit table for each.

## Module: Powers

*Use when characters should have abilities of their own: magic, mutations, psychic gifts, implants, super-powers.*

A **Power** is a defined ability that belongs to the character. Powers are written on the sheet and **take no inventory slots**.

**Using a Power** takes your turn and costs **1 Fatigue**.

- **If you are safe and unhurried,** it simply works. You can also stretch it: a wider area or a longer duration, at no extra cost.
- **If you are in danger or deprived,** make a **WIL save**. Succeed and it works. Fail and it **misfires**: the Warden picks a result that fits, such as harm to you, extra Fatigue, or passing out.

**Affinities.** Powers are grouped into **Affinities**, each a family of related abilities. A character chooses one **Primary Affinity** at creation and learns Powers from it. Affinity is the closest thing this game has to a class: it says what kind of practitioner you are.

**Starting out.** A character who begins with their gift awake knows **2 Powers** from their Primary Affinity. A character may instead begin **latent**, with an Affinity but no Powers, until something in the story wakes it.

**Learning more.** Without the Depth module, a character can know up to 4 Powers, all from their Primary Affinity, gained when the story provides a teacher or an awakening.

**Other costs.** Fatigue suits abilities that wear the body down. A world can swap it for a cost that fits better, keeping everything else the same: charges that refill at rest, a consumable, or a cooldown until the next short rest.

**The world supplies:** what Powers are called and where they come from, a list of Affinities, and about four Powers for each.

## Module: Depth

*Requires Powers. Use for longer campaigns where characters should grow into their abilities.*

There are no experience points. Progress is measured in five **Tiers**.

| Tier | Powers known | Affinities |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Up to 2 | Primary |
| 2 | Up to 4 | Primary |
| 3 | Up to 6 | May begin learning a **Secondary Affinity** |
| 4 | Any number | Primary and Secondary |
| 5 | Any number | Legendary. The Warden and player decide what this means. |

Characters start at Tier 1. The Warden raises a character's Tier when they have done enough that matters: destroyed something that needed destroying, found something lost, protected people, survived what should have killed them. A reasonable pace is one Tier every four or five sessions, slower at the top.

Gaining a Tier raises the limit. The Powers themselves still have to be found or taught in play.

**The world supplies:** names for the Tiers, and the kinds of deeds that earn them.

## Module: Corruption

*Use when the world has something that gets into people: a taint, a plague, radiation, a curse.*

Corruption comes in two forms. A world can use either or both.

**A lasting affliction**

- **Caught by:** taking damage to STR from a corrupted creature or weapon.
- **Each day:** make a WIL save. Count the failures.
- **At 3 failures:** you permanently lose something. With the Depth module, lose one Tier. Without it, lose 1d4 from your maximum WIL.
- **Cured by:** reaching a place of safety the world defines, or a healer with the right Power.
- **The end:** a character who loses everything they have to lose (Tier 0, or WIL 0) is taken by the corruption and becomes the Warden's. Getting them back is a story of its own.

**A hostile field**

- **Caught by:** being near a source of corruption.
- **While affected:** WIL saves to use Powers are made with Disadvantage, and any Fatigue gained from using Powers is **tainted**. Tainted Fatigue is not cleared by rest, only at a place of safety.

**The world supplies:** names for both, what carries them, and what counts as a place of safety.

## Module: Crafting

*Use when gear should become special through the characters' own efforts.*

Ordinary gear can be **tuned** at a special site.

1. Bring one item and one **catalyst** to the site.
2. The crafter makes a **WIL save**.
3. **Succeed:** the item gains a quality. **Fail:** the catalyst is used up, the item is unchanged, and the crafter takes 1 Fatigue.

An item holds one quality. A quality applies in a particular circumstance:

> *Tuned weapon:* attacks are enhanced (d12) in a certain kind of place or against a certain kind of foe.
> *Tuned armor:* +1 to WIL saves against fear and illusion.

If the world has a dark counterpart, items can be tuned that way too, for a harsher effect with a drawback.

**The world supplies:** the sites, the catalysts, and the qualities.

## Module: Tabletop

*Use when playing with miniatures. Without it, the Warden simply describes who is where.*

On your turn you may **move** and **do one thing**. You may give up the one thing to move again.

Pick a **move distance** for the campaign:

| Move | Feel | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| **6 inches** (recommended) | Positioning matters | A kitchen table, wargame terrain |
| **8 inches** | Fast and loose | A large table |
| **6 squares** | Tidy | A grid mat |

- **Melee** needs base contact.
- **Thrown weapons** reach one move. **Bows and guns** reach three moves.
- **Rough ground** counts double.
- **Cover** makes a ranged attack impaired.
- When this book gives a distance in feet, 5 feet is 1 inch or 1 square.

Wardens can set any move distance they like. Roughly one sixth of the table's shorter side works well, and keeping missile range at three moves keeps bows useful without letting them rule the table.
